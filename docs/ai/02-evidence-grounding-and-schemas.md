# 02 — Evidence Grounding, Claim Validation & Schemas (Sprint 37 §2, §8-14, §42-46)

## 1. Hierarchy of Evidence Priority (§10)

The AI layer adheres to a strict five-tier evidence hierarchy. Lower levels cannot override higher levels:

```text
1. Direct Blockchain Facts    (Immutable slot data, authority revocation, pool balances)
       ↓
2. Deterministic Analytics    (Exact holder %, pool depth, tax rates, volume filters)
       ↓
3. Statistical Models         (Wash trading probability, cluster graphs, anomaly z-scores)
       ↓
4. Historical Intelligence    (Creator prior launches, past liquidity drain outcomes)
       ↓
5. AI Natural Language        (Synthesized summaries and contextual explanations)
```

## 3. Claim Validation & Evidence Checking Pipeline (`lib/ai/claim-validator.ts`)

Every statement generated by the AI undergoes an automated grounding audit before presentation to the user:

```text
       Generated AI Claim
               │
               ▼
       Evidence Checker
               │
       Does evidence package support claim?
        ├── YES ──► Display with citation link
        └── NO  ──► Revise to "Insufficient evidence" or replace with verified metric
```

### Unsupported Claim Rule (§44)
If evidence is insufficient to verify an inference (e.g. attributing multi-sig ownership to a single real-world person without cluster proof), the system explicitly replaces the claim with:
> *"Insufficient evidence to determine whether these wallets are controlled by the same entity."*

---

## 4. Confidence vs. Certainty (§11-12)

Every important AI finding displays an explicit confidence level:
- **HIGH CONFIDENCE**: Strong deterministic or multi-source evidence (e.g. creator controls 18% of effective supply).
- **MEDIUM CONFIDENCE**: Statistical correlation or cluster heuristic (e.g. wallets appear behaviorally linked).
- **LOW CONFIDENCE**: Preliminary observation with limited sample size.

> [!WARNING]
> **Confidence is Not Certainty**: A confidence score of `92%` means **92% confidence in the reliability of the analysis given available evidence** — it NEVER means a *"92% chance this token will succeed"*.
